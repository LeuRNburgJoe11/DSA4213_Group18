Applying the deck's project template (slide 41) — "We want someone to ___. Our agents will ___. Our contribution is ___. We will demonstrate and evaluate it through ___." — to each of these three directions:

Section 2 — Autonomous Research

(reference: AI Scientist, Lu et al. 2024)

We want someone to hand the agent an open question about an NLP model's behavior — not a fixed dataset task, but something like "does this model's performance degrade under X perturbation, and why?"
Our agents will propose an experiment, write and run the code, inspect the results, and decide whether to follow up, abandon the line of inquiry, or attempt to reproduce a surprising result.
Our contribution is the mechanism the agent uses to pick informative next experiments (rather than random ones) and/or a self-check step that catches misread results before they become claims.
We will demonstrate and evaluate it through a research notebook showing the full decision trail, and a final report where every claim links back to the run, code, and figure that produced it. Evidence to collect: reproducibility of runs, whether claims actually hold up under re-execution.
Section 5 — Embodied Action and Skill Learning

(reference: Voyager and SayCan — note this doesn't require physical robots; both the slides' project idea and Voyager itself run in a simulated/game environment, so it fits your "no physical research" constraint)

We want someone to watch an agent tackle a task it can't yet do, in a game or simulated world with visible state and actions.
Our agents will attempt the task, extract a reusable skill from a successful attempt, store it in a skill library, and later call on that skill as a building block for a harder goal.
Our contribution is the logic for what to learn next, how a skill gets verified before being saved (so bad skills don't pollute the library), or how skills compose into longer plans.
We will demonstrate and evaluate it through an inspectable skill library the audience can browse, a replay of how each skill was learned, and a transfer test — moving the agent to a new but related setting to see which skills carry over.
Section 6 — Agent Security

(reference: AgentDojo, Debenedetti et al., NeurIPS 2024)

We want someone to give the agent a legitimate task (e.g., "read these documents and summarize/act on them") while an attacker embeds competing instructions inside the content the agent has to read.
Our agents will process untrusted content through tools while distinguishing the user's actual instructions from injected ones, deciding which actions are authorized.
Our contribution is a specific defense mechanism, a new attack strategy that exposes a gap in existing defenses, or a benchmark targeting an underexplored injection vector.
We will demonstrate and evaluate it through paired metrics — attack success rate and task utility — replaying attempts to show exactly where an attack was blocked (or got through) and confirming the original task still got done.

Given your stated interest, Section 2 + Section 6 combined (a research agent that has to detect and resist injected/adversarial content while doing its research loop) is still the most novel angle — it's not just AgentDojo's email-agent setting, and it avoids the physical-robot territory of Section 5 entirely if you'd rather drop that one.
